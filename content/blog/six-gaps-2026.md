Over the years, many MIT cyclists have taken on the challenging “6 gaps” route in Vermont, covering 130 miles and 11,000+ ft of elevation over six passes in the Green Mountains. This Labor day weekend, the Three Musketeers, Lorenzo, Martin, and Sparrow, made a rendezvous in Bethel, VT to attempt this epic ride and celebrate the end of the summer. We also anticipated that the steep grades and sections of unpaved roads would be better suited to gravel bikes with low gearing and wide tires, and we felt that ours absolutely crushed the course. (Lorenzo ran a 42T chainring with a 10-50T cassette. Martin ran a 40T chainring with 10-44T cassette. Sparrow ran a 40T chainring with 11-44T cassette.). Although the trip was planned around the full-day ride, the weather led us to split it up into two days as a delightful training weekend, in contrast to previous editions overwhelmed with type II fun.

![](/img/blog/posts/six-gaps-2026/image11.jpg)
_Lorenzo and Martin ready to roll to Vermont with their gravel bikes_

On Saturday, we woke up at 6 to start our ride up Rochester gap in cool weather with overcast skies. We were buzzing with energy and looking forward to our biggest climb since Mt Lemmon  at WTC. As we warmed up the legs on the shallow slopes, a car passed us, did a u-turn, and passed us again while holding a MIT cap to cheer us along! Thrilled by the encouragement, we rode together up some painfully steep ramps while trying to convince ourselves that they weren’t really that bad at all.

![](/img/blog/posts/six-gaps-2026/image1.jpg)
_First gap done! All smiles :)_

When we began the descent of Rochester gap, we had the only mechanical of the whole trip: a puncture that didn’t seal until we plugged it. After this, we took the twisty descent to Rochester with caution to keep our speed under control and on our arrival to the town were greeted by friendly drivers and majestic views of the Green Mountains.

![](/img/blog/posts/six-gaps-2026/image2.jpg)
_Majestic scenery in the Green Mountains_

Our next climb was Brandon gap and while its grade was the most gentle of all the climbs, the effort was not. Sparrow rode ahead at his sweet spot while Lorenzo saved energy at an endurance pace, with Martin somewhere in between. Everyone’s unique approach to the climb reflected their willingness to take great risks for even greater rewards on such a long day on the bike. As we crested the climb, we noticed a drizzle had begun and we proceeded to take the wet descent with caution. Down in the valley, the light and persistent rain soaked through our socks and shoes, so we took a break at a general store to warm up and refuel.

We began Middlebury gap in a better state and worked our way to the top more urgently than the previous gap because of the rain on our backs. This climb started to eat away our confidence about tackling the full route, not only because of the pain-inducing ramps towards the top but also because of the cold and wet descent. As we reached Hancock, we stopped at their general store for lunch and considered our options. We hadn’t even done half the distance of the ride and with the three hardest climbs left we weren’t feeling optimistic about completing the route due to the weather. So, we took the easy way out and decided to ride back to the airbnb so we could recover and finish the last three gaps the next day.

![](/img/blog/posts/six-gaps-2026/image4.jpg)
_Three Musketeers frolicking on Vermont pavement_

En route from Hancock to Rochester, the rain lifted and our mood bounced back as we cruised on the valley road. We just enjoyed the scenery as our thoughts turned towards good post-ride meals and maple creemees. As we passed one particular farm, we saw two gun-slinging Vermonters with rifles across their backs, which motivated us to ride even faster. Once in Rochester, we retraced our route by climbing the gap in reverse, which thankfully wasn’t as leg-busting as the other side.

We enjoyed the evening after the ride by visiting Randolph to eat maple creemees and feast at the local tavern. Crowds of GMSR racers were also flocking around the town and led to some lively discussions at the local bike shop. Since we had another day of riding ahead of us, we also restocked our ride snacks, or just sugar and electrolytes for our water. That night, we went to sleep peacefully by counting the sheep in the backyard of the airbnb where we stayed.

![](/img/blog/posts/six-gaps-2026/image9.jpg)
_Our sheepish friends ... baaaaa_

![](/img/blog/posts/six-gaps-2026/image8.jpg)
_Sparrow adjusting his saddle angle with a baking tray to reduce climbing-induced back pain_

We started our second day by driving to Warren and ascending the formidable Lincoln gap, known for having the steepest paved mile in the U.S. Compared to the day before, the climbs were shorter, perhaps only twenty minutes each, but all the more brutal. Going up Lincoln gap can only be described as fighting for your life up a 20+ % grade. It was mandatory to grind up the climb in the lowest gear while also struggling to balance the bike and possibly zig-zag to prolong the suffering in exchange for taking off the sharp edge. Whoever designed the road clearly intended to induce despair by making it go straight up so that you could see exactly how far you had to go. At the top, hikers congratulated us on the valiant effort while we basked in post hill-climb relief.

![](/img/blog/posts/six-gaps-2026/image10.jpg)
_A mandatory stop at the Warren covered bridge_

![](/img/blog/posts/six-gaps-2026/image3.jpg)
_The highest point of the trip at the top of Lincoln gap_

We forged onwards towards App gap, where we expected to cross paths with the GMSR stage of the day. This climb was especially race-worthy, ascending multiple switchbacks while slowly but surely getting steeper towards the top. Feeling inspired by the race, we decided to go full gas on the climb. It was magical how as we climbed, a fog enveloped the top of the climb making only several feet of the road visible ahead. We were urged up and onwards by cheering spectators and words of encouragement written in chalk on the ground. The barriers appeared out of the mist like on a grand tour stage to signal that we had one final steep pitch to wrestle up. At the top, we stayed to watch racers roll in and congratulated them on their efforts.

![](/img/blog/posts/six-gaps-2026/image5.jpg)
_The foggy GMSR App gap stage finish line_

Eventually, we descended the other side, returning to the warm and sunny valley to enjoy a lunch break. An excellent bagel and donut stop was on our way, which left us happy and refueled to continue back to Warren for our last climb. There was a fair bit of hilly riding to get there, which we took rather easily so we could chat and enjoy the scenery. Somewhere along the way, we saw another gun-slinging Vermont state trooper in full military gear, so we hurried again to get far away from their sting.

Our last gap of the trip was Roxbury gap and the sun was really baking overhead on the climb. We all focused on the now-familiar sight of the steep roads and made quick work of the climb before it got too hot. At the top, we were treated to splendid views of both valleys before we turned around to go to our car. The drive home wouldn’t have been complete without a creemee, which we secured and gleefully ate to celebrate our accomplishment of climbing all six gaps.

![](/img/blog/posts/six-gaps-2026/image7.jpg)
_Martin and Sparrow summit Roxbury gap to finish all six_

After all, we were grateful that the weather made us split the epic ride into two days. We all caught the Vermont bug and are looking forward to more climbs in the Green mountains, and perhaps racing GMSR next year!
